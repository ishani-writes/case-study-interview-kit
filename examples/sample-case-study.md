# Sample case study (fictional)

Lumen Freight and Tidewater Analytics are invented for this example. Every name, quote and number below is made up to show the format and the level of detail.

# "We stopped finding out about late deliveries from our customers."

Lumen Freight's dispatch team tracked every shipment in a spreadsheet and learned about delays when customers called. After moving risk tracking to Tidewater Analytics, dispatchers see at-risk shipments before the customer does and reroute drivers the same morning.

## The challenge

Every morning, Lumen's dispatchers opened a shared spreadsheet with a few hundred shipments and started calling drivers. By the time they found a late delivery, the customer had often found it first.

"Most of my mornings went to chasing drivers by phone before I could answer a single customer," says Priya Rao, Head of Operations. The cost showed up in two places: dispatcher hours spent on calls instead of planning, and customers who started to doubt the delivery windows they had been promised. Lumen had tried adding color-coding and a few scripts to the spreadsheet, but the sheet could only show a delay after it had happened.

## What they were looking for

Priya had three requirements, in this order. The team had to learn it in an afternoon, because dispatchers had no time for a training project. It had to warn them before the customer noticed, not after. And it had to work with the phones their drivers already carried, with no new hardware.

## Why they chose Tidewater

Lumen looked at a large fleet-management suite and at extending the spreadsheet with more scripts. The suite promised everything and needed a long rollout. The scripts had already hit their limit.

They ran a two-week pilot at one depot. On day six, the tool flagged a delivery as at risk about three hours before the customer would have noticed. Dev Malhotra, the dispatch lead, rerouted a driver and the customer got a heads-up instead of a complaint. "That was the day the dispatchers stopped asking whether it would work," Dev says. Tidewater's implementation lead also built the driver text-message flow during the pilot week, which removed the last objection about hardware.

## What they built

Dispatchers use three features, and they use them in this order of frequency.

**The at-risk list.** Every dispatcher opens it first thing in the morning. It ranks shipments by how likely they are to be late, so the team calls the riskiest drivers first. Dev says the list replaced the spreadsheet within the first week, without anyone being told to stop using it.

**Driver messaging.** Dispatchers send a short text from a set of templates, such as "Traffic on Route 9, can you confirm your ETA?" Drivers reply by text, and the reply is logged against the shipment. The team now rarely needs to call.

**The weekly delay report.** Priya reviews it every Monday with the depot leads. It shows which routes and time windows produced the most delays, and the leads use it to adjust schedules.

"My team calls the driver first now, and the customer hears from us before they have to ask," Priya says.

## What surprised them

Two things. First, the spreadsheet disappeared on its own. Nobody had to be convinced to give it up.

Second, the first version of the at-risk list flagged far too many shipments. Dispatchers started ignoring it. Dev and Tidewater's support lead spent two weeks tuning the thresholds, and the list became useful once it flagged fewer shipments with more accuracy. Dev says he would have liked a warning that tuning would take that long.

## What's next

Lumen is bringing its second depot onto the system next quarter. Priya also wants to start sending customers a delay notice before they have to ask, using the same flags the dispatchers see. The Monday delay report will move into the monthly operations review so the findings feed schedule changes.

## Who made it happen

At Lumen, Dev Malhotra owned the rollout and the threshold tuning, and Priya Rao sponsored the pilot and ran the review each week. Twelve dispatchers changed how they start their day. At Tidewater, an implementation lead built the driver text-message flow in a week, and a support lead worked with Dev on tuning. Lumen's team did the change. Tidewater supplied the tooling and the help.
